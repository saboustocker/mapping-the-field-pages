Appendix D. Impact of Within- and Across-Cluster Citation and Early Adoption on Citeability within and beyond Corpus

Introduction

  • Two questions on what predicts how often a corpus paper is cited.
  • Early adoption: does entering a technology cluster early pay off?
  • Citation breadth: does citing within one’s own cluster or across clusters pay off?
  • Each estimated overall, across all 16 technology clusters, and separately per cluster.
  • Descriptive and observational; no causal identification.

Shared Setup

Method

  • Sample: corpus papers present in the within-corpus citation network, each with a technology cluster and a publication year.
  • Two outcomes, reported side by side:
    • Within Corpus — local citations, citations received from other corpus articles.
    • Total — global citations, citations received from anywhere.
  • Model family: negative-binomial GAM (mgcv::gam, family = nb()), appropriate for over-dispersed counts.
  • Publication age (age = 2026 − PY) entered as a smooth, so no decay shape is assumed.
  • Age is discrete; the basis dimension is capped below the number of distinct ages available.
  • Overall models additionally control for cluster citeability, the mean global citations of the paper’s cluster.
  • Per-cluster models omit that control, since it is constant within a cluster.
  • Coefficients are on the log scale of the expected citation count.
  • Code: 30_academic_foundation/07_citation_network.R.

Early Adoption

Method

  • Predictor: early adoption score = publication year − year of the earliest paper in the same technology cluster.
  • Lower score = earlier entry into the cluster; 0 = the cluster’s first paper.
  • Negative coefficient = earlier papers accumulate more citations.
  • Overall specification: citations ~ early adoption score + cluster citeability + s(age).
  • Per-cluster specification: citations ~ early adoption score, fitted separately for each of the 16 clusters.
  • Output: impact_early_adoption_per_cluster.csv / .md.

Citing Within and Across Technology Clusters

Method

  • Two predictors, both counted on citations the paper makes, not receives:
    • wi — citations to papers of its own technology cluster.
    • ac — citations to papers of other technology clusters.
  • Both standardised by opportunity, since early papers have fewer papers available to cite:
    • wi divided by the papers published in the same cluster before that year.
    • ac divided by the papers published in all other clusters before that year.
  • Papers with an undefined standardised score, that is no prior papers to cite, are dropped.
  • Overall specification: citations ~ wi + ac + cluster citeability + s(age).
  • Per-cluster specification: citations ~ wi + ac + s(age), fitted separately for each of the 16 clusters.
  • Output: impact_citations_per_cluster.csv / .md.
