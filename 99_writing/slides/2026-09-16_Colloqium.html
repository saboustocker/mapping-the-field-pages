Research \(\rightleftarrows\) Impact


  1. Speed and salience within the field of consumer technology research

    \(\rightarrow\) Research has limited attention to allocate: we need to understand where it went to in the past and where it is going now

  2. Interconnectedness and intellectual foundation

    \(\rightarrow\) Research builds on cumulative theory: we need to understand if we treat Generative AI as an integrated next step in consumer technology research, or if we study it in isolation

  3. Alignment with public salience

    \(\rightarrow\) Research has value to offer: we need to focus our attention on what matters to the beneficiaries of our research

Mapping the Field

Mapping a Field?

  • Bibliometric analyses: dominant authors, journals, keywords, co-occurences, …
  • Semantic analyses and topic modelling: using embedding models to quantify and relate terms to each other
  • When “mapping a field”, one may think of different approaches, but many

Our Approach

Inclusion and Exclusion

  • 7 leading marketing journals
  • Retreival of data using SCOPUS and Web of Science
  • Inclusion criteria: consumer technology (Hoffman et al., 2022), empirical, published between January 1st, 2000 – June 30th, 2026
  • LLM-based approach: positive & negative prompts, human verification

Identify consumer technology research

Semantic Modelling and Clustering

  • Create embeddings from all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (Sentence Transformers, 2026) (\(384D\))
  • Deduplicate terms and collapse terms with cosine similarity \(\geq 0.9\)
  • Dimensionality reduction to \(10D\) using UMAP (McInnes et al., 2018)
  • k-means clustering (\(k = 10\)) and manual label of clusters
  • Generalized additive models (Wood, 2017): peak years for each cluster on smooth of age

\(\Rightarrow\) Decision on embedding model, \(D\) reduction and \(k\) based on combined evaluation of statistical optimization and interpretability

Bibliometric Analysis

  • Historiographic analysis within corpus (Aria & Cuccurullo, 2017; Garfield, 2004)
  • Co-citation analysis (Aria & Cuccurullo, 2017; Small, 1980)
    • Pure method papers manually removed before clustering
    • 500 most cited references
    • Louvain community detection (minimum edge weight = 3)
  • Each reference node coloured by the technology cluster dominating its citing articles (threshold \(\geq 25\%\))
  • Analysis of influence of journals

Benchmarking against Public Attention

  • Construction of external benchmark with MediaCloud (Bermejo et al., 2026; Roberts et al., 2021), US News Collection (247 news sources)
    • Data from January 1st, 2008 – June 30th, 2026
  • Gap analysis (relative attention to each consumer technology cluster)
  • Lag analysis (\(-5\) to \(+5\) lags) with exact two-sided permutation tests

Benchmarking against Public Attention

  • More specific = more academic attention
  • Rank order gaps \(\rightleftarrows\) percentage gaps
  • Exact permutation tests on cross-correlated lags (media shifted circularly against academia)
  • Much noise = difficulty determining systematic relationships
  • High positive at positive lag, media rises and academic attention rises some years later
  • High positive at negative lag, academic rises and media coverage follows some years later
  • High negative at positive lag, media coverage rises and academic attention falls afterwards, the two move in opposite directions with media first
  • High negative at negative lag, academic attention rises and media coverage falls afterwards, opposite movement with academia first
  • High monotone: It means the two series share a slow common trend rather than a datable lead-lag relation. If correlation stays near the same high value at every lag, shifting by five years in either direction barely changes the fit, so no lag is identified — the flat curve carries no timing information.

Limitations

  1. Top 7 journals in marketing \(\neq\) academia, and marketing journals \(\neq\) comprehensive source of research on AI
  2. Bottom-up semantic analysis \(\neq\) theory-driven, data-agnostic framework
  3. All data is correlational: we can not and do not make causal claims

Implications


For Researchers

  1. Normalized technology in academia \(\neq\) loss of interest in the public domain
  2. If you study Generative AI, build on an inclusive foundation

For Journals and Their Editors

  1. Don’t chase novelty at the cost of impact
  2. Assess submissions on how they build cumulative understanding
  3. Consider the theoretical and practical foundation of empirical work
