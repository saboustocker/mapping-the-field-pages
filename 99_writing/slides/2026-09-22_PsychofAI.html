(Moschella, 2025; Puntoni, 2026; S. Puntoni, 2026; Zachary, 2026)

  • Over the past quarter-century, research on consumer technologies has increased, to the extent that in 2026, 40% of publications in the top seven marketing journals are empirical papers concerning consumer technologies to some extent
  • The increased interest in CT is not as new, but in 2022, something happened
  • Ever since, we hear a lot of voices from both academics, the public, and practitioners, that Generative AI, claiming that Generative AI fundamentally disrupts our research practices, consumers’ experiences, and marketers’ strategies

The Evolving Field of Consumer Technology Research


  • more research on consumer technologies (de Bellis et al., 2026; Hoffman et al., 2022; Kahn & Wilson, 2025)
  • faster pace of consumer technologies being adopted (Hoffman et al., 2022)


  • AI as the latest technology in line:
    • increase of publications on AI (Riandhi et al., 2025)
    • increase of submissions with AI (Gartenberg et al., 2026)
    • increase of editorials and special issues on topic (Bart et al., 2026; Wang, 2026)

Consumer technologies as a research object have been gaining traction, with Generative AI latest in line proclaiming to disrupt consumers’ and researchers’ lives alike.

But does it also disrupt how we treat consumer technologies as a research object?

Why should we care?


Attention Allocation – Relevance

  • limited attention in a fast-moving field (Hoffman et al., 2022)
  • different pressures shaping the space (Ellison, 2002; Gartenberg et al., 2026; Harrison et al., 2017)

Intellectual Connection – Accumulation

  • cumulative vs. isolated clusters of research (Aria & Cuccurullo, 2017)
  • shared vs. specialized intellectual foundation / theorizing (Small, 1980)

Public Concern – Impact

  • overall alignment with public concern (de Bellis et al., 2026; Hoffman et al., 2022)
  • temporal alignment with public concern (de Bellis et al., 2026; Hoffman et al., 2022)

Objectives and Research Questions

1

How has consumer research allocated attention across consumer technologies over time?

2

How is research on different consumer technologies intellectually connected?

3

How does academic attention to consumer technologies align with what concerns the public?


… and does research on Generative AI differ from these patterns?

3 – Contrasting the Field

Gap Analysis

Temporal Lags between Academic and Public Attention

  • High positive at positive lag, media rises and academic attention rises some years later
  • High positive at negative lag, academic rises and media attention follows some years later
  • High negative at positive lag, media attention rises and academic attention falls afterwards, the two move in opposite directions with media first
  • High negative at negative lag, academic attention rises and media attention falls afterwards, opposite movement with academia first
  • High monotone: It means the two series share a slow common trend rather than a datable lead-lag relation. If correlation stays near the same high value at every lag, shifting by five years in either direction barely changes the fit, so no lag is identified — the flat curve carries no timing information.

Implications

Consumer Technology Research at Large

For Researchers

  1. Learn from The Field

\(\rightarrow\) Study new technologies through an informed lense from earlier technologies

\(\rightarrow\) consider the established theoretical foundation before building your own

  1. Study technologies as part of the Map

\(\rightarrow\) Many new consumer technologies do not exist in isolation, study them accordingly

  1. Take public concern into account

\(\rightarrow\) Normalized technologies \(\neq\) lack of relevance

\(\rightarrow\) Use the opportunity to further understand normalized technologies

For Journals & their Editors

  1. Value Cumulative Theorizing

\(\rightarrow\) value research building on what is known

  1. Value Integrative Research

\(\rightarrow\) Higlight research trying to understand consumer technologies as an integrated concept

  1. Mind the Gap

\(\rightarrow\) Specialization or generalization as conscious editorial decisions

\(\rightarrow\) Alignment or divergence from public concern as conscious editorial decisions

Implications

The Case of Generative AI

For Researchers

  1. Reciprocate Connections

\(\rightarrow\) learn and reference from other technologies

  1. Move beyond acceptance

\(\rightarrow\) Expand to richer theorizing

  1. Respect the Generalizeability of Generative AI

\(\rightarrow\) Conduct integrative research (including usage)

For Journals & their Editors

  1. Adapt the agenda

\(\rightarrow\) Research on Generative AI is published everyhwere, the question turn to what kind of research is published where

  1. Novelty alone is no longer a gap

\(\rightarrow\) Publications should go beyond Generative AI adoption

  1. Treat media attention with caution

\(\rightarrow\) Academia overvalues Generative AI

\(\rightarrow\) no need to “catch up”, but need to understand impact

Conclusion


1

Attention moves in waves

Generative AI is the biggest one yet.

2

The field is intellectually connected

Generative AI is not yet.

3

Academic and public attention rarely align.

For Generative AI they do.

Our Approach (2/4)

Semantic Modeling and Clustering

  • Create embeddings from all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (Sentence Transformers, 2026) (\(384D\))
  • Deduplicate terms and collapse terms with cosine similarity \(\geq 0.9\)
  • Dimensionality reduction to \(10D\) using UMAP (McInnes et al., 2018)
  • k-means clustering (\(k = 16\)) and manual label of clusters
  • Generalized additive models (Wood, 2017): peak years for each cluster on smooth of age

\(\Rightarrow\) Decision on embedding model, \(D\) reduction and \(k\) based on combined evaluation of statistical optimization and interpretability
